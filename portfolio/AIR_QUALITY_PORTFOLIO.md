# 5. Project Overview

### Problem Statement
The National Capital Region (NCR) of Delhi faces some of the world's most severe seasonal air pollution crises. During winter months (November to January), meteorological inversion, collapsing planetary boundary layer heights (<300m), near-calm surface winds (<0.8 m/s), and regional emission sources converge to push ambient Air Quality Index (AQI) into the "Severe" and "Severe+" hazardous categories ($AQI \ge 400$).

While municipal authorities rely on the Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM) Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP) to enact emergency interventions (such as halting construction, banning heavy diesel vehicles, and closing schools), enforcement is frequently **reactive**. Statutory actions are often triggered only after severe pollution has already blanketed the city for multiple hours.

### Project Motivation & Goals
1. **Actionable Multi-Horizon Early Warning:** Develop machine learning models that forecast continuous AQI at 1-hour (immediate alert), 6-hour (intra-day planning), and 24-hour (statutory next-day GRAP staging) horizons across Delhi's primary monitoring stations.
2. **Robust Data Engineering:** Build an end-to-end, idempotent relational pipeline in PostgreSQL 16 capable of ingesting raw government telemetry, standardizing sparse sensor streams, and tracking full source lineage.
3. **Overcoming ML Failure Modes:** Diagnose and resolve tree-based model extrapolation collapse during unprecedented winter pollution crises.
4. **Cost-Sensitive Decision Theory:** Bridge the gap between continuous regression outputs and real-world policy trade-offs by optimizing decision thresholds under asymmetric public health loss penalties.
5. **Rigorous Causal Policy Evaluation:** Apply quasi-experimental econometric methods (Interrupted Time Series, pre-trend falsification, and combustion tracer analysis) to evaluate whether Delhi's Diwali firecracker blanket ban produced a statistically measurable reduction in ambient AQI.

- **Primary Particulates:** $\text{PM}_{2.5}$ ($\mu\text{g/m}^3$), $\text{PM}_{10}$ ($\mu\text{g/m}^3$).
- **Gaseous Precursors:** Nitric Oxide ($\text{NO}$), Nitrogen Dioxide ($\text{NO}_2$), Oxides of Nitrogen ($\text{NO}_x$), Ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$), Sulfur Dioxide ($\text{SO}_2$), Carbon Monoxide ($\text{CO}$), Ozone ($\text{O}_3$).
- **Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs):** Benzene, Toluene, Ethylbenzene, ortho-Xylene, MP-Xylene.
- **Meteorological Sensors:** Ambient Temperature ($\text{AT}$, $^\circ\text{C}$), Relative Humidity ($\text{RH}$, $\%$), Wind Speed ($\text{WS}$, $\text{m/s}$), Wind Direction ($\text{WD}$, $\text{deg}$), Solar Radiation ($\text{SR}$, $\text{W/m}^2$), Barometric Pressure ($\text{BP}$, $\text{mmHg}$), Vertical Wind Speed ($\text{VWS}$, $\text{m/s}$), Rainfall ($\text{RF}$, $\text{mm}$).

### Data Quality & QC Hardening
1. **Sentinel Value Disambiguation:** Traditional cleaning scripts often nullify digits like `9` or `-9`. Our pipeline discovered that legitimate winter temperatures (e.g., $9^\circ\text{C}$) were being inadvertently dropped. Sentinels were strictly isolated to extreme physical constants (`-999`, `-9999`, `9999`).
2. **Barometric Pressure Bounds Trapping:** Detected faulty instrument clipping at $999.0\ \text{mmHg}$. Validation logic strictly bounded valid barometric pressure to $(400.0, 998.9)\ \text{mmHg}$ to protect true local atmospheric pressure signals.
3. **Cell-Level Nullification with JSONB Audit Trail:** Rather than discarding entire measurement rows when a single sensor malfunctions, individual invalid cells are set to `NULL` while preserving all valid companion sensors. Every nullification is logged into a dedicated PostgreSQL `qc_flags` JSONB column (e.g., `{"pm25": "SENTINEL_-999"}`).
4. **Filename Regex Date Reconstruction:** Resolved an upstream government Excel formatting quirk where AQI sheets recorded days as bare integers ($1, 2, \dots, 31$); filenames were parsed with regex (`YYYY-Month`) and joined with day integers to construct unambiguous `Asia/Kolkata` timestamps.
5. **Source Lineage:** Tracked 105 individual source files with cryptographic SHA-256 hashes, file modification metadata, and ingested row counts in a dedicated `source_files` table.

### Key Engineering Decisions
- **Idempotent Upsert Logic:** Fact tables enforce `UNIQUE(station_id, timestamp)` constraints. The Python ETL pipeline executes vectorized SQL batch upserts (`ON CONFLICT (station_id, timestamp) DO UPDATE ...`), guaranteeing that running the ingestion pipeline repeatedly produces identical, clean state without duplicate rows.
- **Timezone Integrity:** All timestamps are strictly parsed and stored as `TIMESTAMPTZ` set to Indian Standard Time (`Asia/Kolkata`, UTC+05:30), preventing UTC shift artifacts during daylight-saving or server migrations.
- **Sensor Sparsity Handling:** Engineered the schema to handle heterogeneous hardware configurations gracefully (e.g., ITO possesses no meteorological sensors; Dwarka lacks ozone sensors; rainfall is 56.6% sparse across the network).

### Strict Zero-Data-Leakage Enforcement
1. **Strictly Backward-Looking Windows:** All rolling statistics use right-closed windows $[t-W+1, t]$ or explicit 1-hour lag offsets (`closed='right'`), ensuring future timestamps never enter feature calculations.
2. **Leave-One-Out Spatial Aggregation:** Station-to-station network averages strictly exclude the target station itself ($s' \neq s$) and operate on $t-1$ observations to prevent contemporaneous target leakage.
3. **Chronological Splitting & Warm-Up Buffers:**
   - **Training Set:** Jan 01, 2025 – Aug 31, 2025 (~8 months, 67% of data: Winter, Summer, early Monsoon).
   - **Validation Set:** Sep 01, 2025 – Oct 31, 2025 (~2 months, 16.5% of data: late Monsoon & Post-Monsoon transition; used for Optuna tuning).
   - **Test Set:** Nov 01, 2025 – Dec 31, 2025 (~2 months, 16.5% of data: peak Winter severe pollution crisis).
   - 2024 records served as a warm-up buffer for rolling/lag features, preventing missing values at the start of 2025.
   - All scalers, imputers, and encoders were fit strictly on the Training split and applied out-of-sample.

# 9. Forecasting

Models were developed, tuned on validation, and evaluated once on the peak-winter test set (Nov–Dec 2025, 7 stations). Because AQI is a 24-hour rolling average (lag-1 autocorrelation ≈ 0.998), naive persistence is a very strong baseline, so every model is reported next to it with a skill score `1 − metric_model / metric_persistence`.

| :---: | :--- | ---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **1-Hour** | Tuned LightGBM | 10,057 | 2.29 | 5.08 | 0.992 | 2.40 | 3.82 | 0.995 | +0.047 | **−0.332** |

### Horizon Deep-Dives & Key Modeling Decisions
- **1-Hour Ahead ($h=1$):**
  - *Intended Role:* Short-term alerting.
  - *Dynamics:* Dominated by persistence ($r \approx 0.998$). LightGBM has slightly lower MAE (2.29 vs 2.40) but **loses to persistence on RMSE and $R^2$**, so there is no real 1h skill. The high $R^2$ reflects the smoothed target, not the model.

# 10. Model Failure → Redesign

A central technical milestone in this project was the diagnosis and architectural redesign of the 24-hour forecasting model, which initially failed catastrophically before being re-engineered into a winning hybrid architecture.

### The Initial Failure (MAE = 98.24)
When standard gradient-boosted decision trees (XGBoost / LightGBM) were trained on standard features and evaluated on the peak winter test set (Nov–Dec 2025), the 24h model collapsed:
- **Initial Test MAE:** `98.24` (drastically worse than naive persistence MAE of `38.34`).
- **Peak Crisis Error:** On test samples where actual AQI exceeded 400 (mean actual AQI = $429.49$), the tree model predicted a mean of only $293.01$ — an underprediction bias of **$-136.48$ AQI points**.

### Root Cause Diagnosis
1. **Tree-Based Extrapolation Ceiling:** Decision trees partition feature space with orthogonal step functions. They cannot extrapolate beyond the maximum target value seen during training ($\max \hat{y} = 347.61$). When unprecedented winter inversion trapped pollution above 450 AQI, the tree was mathematically incapable of predicting those values.
2. **Non-Stationary Calendar Overfitting:** Ordinal temporal features (`month`, `day_of_year`) created high-gain tree splits that memorized training-set calendar patterns. When evaluated on unseen winter dynamics, samples were erroneously routed into clean monsoon leaf nodes ($\hat{y} \approx 85$).

### The Hybrid Redesign & Solution
1. **Feature Pruning & Long-Horizon Curation:** Stripped non-stationary calendar variables. Added multi-day particulate lags (`pm25_lag_48h`, `pm25_lag_72h`, `pm25_lag_168h`), multi-day causal rolling windows, and leave-one-out spatial network aggregates (49 curated causal features).
2. **Continuous Unbounded Extrapolation:** Replaced decision trees with an L2-regularized **Ridge Regression** model ($\alpha = 1000$). The linear formulation provides continuous, unbounded slope extrapolation that tracks severe pollution spikes without clipping.
3. **50/50 Hybrid Blending:** Formed an ensemble combining 50% Naive Persistence (retaining local autoregressive memory) with 50% Ridge Regression (injecting multi-day trend and spatial network signals).
4. **Outcome:** MAE dropped from **98.24 to 34.11** ($R^2$ rose to **0.365**), beating persistence (38.34) by 4.23 AQI points on average (MAE skill +0.11).

# 12. Cost-Sensitive Threshold Optimization

Classification errors have asymmetric costs:
- **False Negative (Missed Severe hour):** unmitigated public exposure.
- **False Positive (Precautionary false alarm):** temporary economic cost (e.g., road sweeping, pausing construction).

### Asymmetric Decision Optimization Formulation
With a 5:1 loss ratio ($C_{\text{FN}} = 5 \cdot C_{\text{FP}}$), the threshold is chosen by minimising expected cost **on the validation set (Sep–Oct 2025)** and then applied once to the test set:
$$\tau^* = \arg\min_{\tau} \sum_{i \in \text{val}} \left[ C_{\text{FN}} \cdot \mathbb{I}(y_i \ge 401 \land \hat{y}_i < \tau) + C_{\text{FP}} \cdot \mathbb{I}(y_i < 401 \land \hat{y}_i \ge \tau) \right]$$

An earlier version selected this threshold on the test set (341.5 AQI, "95.7% recall"); that leakage was removed and those numbers are no longer valid.

### Results (24-Hour Lead Time)

Source: `reports/classification/metrics/cost_optimal_thresholds_summary.csv` and `threshold_operating_points_comparison.csv`.

| Operating Policy | Cutoff (AQI) | Val Severe Recall | Val Severe Precision | Test Severe Recall | Test Severe Precision | Test Missed Severe Hours | Test False Alarm Hours |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Statutory Fixed CPCB** | 401.0 | 20.8% | 32.2% | 61.08% | 73.86% | 1,568 h | 871 h |
| **Validation-optimal (5:1)** | 274.5 | 100.0% | 9.81% | 99.83% | 42.93% | 7 h | 5,347 h |

# 13. Diwali Causal Analysis

**Framing:** why naive before/after comparisons mislead. The question was whether Delhi's firecracker ban during Diwali measurably reduced ambient pollution. The ban was active in every year studied, so there is no untreated comparison year, and **a ban effect is not identifiable with this data**. The finding is methodological: naive designs produce large, spurious "effects", which the pre-trend and placebo checks expose.

### Identification Strategy & Methodological Setup
- **Scope:** 7 DPCC/CPCB monitoring stations across a $\pm 14$-day window around Diwali 2025 (October 06 to November 03, 2025; Diwali on October 20, 2025).
- **Inference:** Standard errors clustered by date (28 clusters), because stations on the same day share weather and are strongly correlated.
- **Pre-Trends Test:** Tested for a pre-existing trend ($H_0: \beta_{\text{pre}} = 0$) over the 14 days before the festival.
- **Regressions:** Naive OLS, station fixed effects, meteorological controls, Interrupted Time Series (ITS), and an in-time placebo. Raw daily PM2.5 is also modelled because AQI is pre-smoothed.

### Regression Findings (AQI outcome)

Source: `reports/causal/regression_models_summary.csv` (`se_type = cluster_by_date`).

| Model Specification | Estimated Effect ($\beta$) | Clustered SE | $p$-value | 95% Conf. Interval | $R^2$ | Interpretation |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Pre-Trend Linear Drift** | $+20.38\ \text{pts/day}$ | $2.25$ | $< 0.001$ | $[+15.97, +24.78]$ | $0.770$ | AQI was already rising fast before Diwali; naive pre/post is invalid. |
| **Model 1: Naive Before/After** | $+135.57$ | $24.78$ | $< 0.001$ | $[+87.01, +184.13]$ | $0.465$ | Confounded by the seasonal trajectory. |
| **Model 2: Station Fixed Effects** | $+136.35$ | $24.92$ | $< 0.001$ | $[+87.50, +185.20]$ | $0.528$ | Absorbs station baselines; still confounded. |
| **Model 3: Meteo Controls + FE** | $+114.53$ | $18.24$ | $< 0.001$ | $[+78.79, +150.28]$ | $0.722$ | Surface weather only; still confounded by trend. |
| **Model 4: Interrupted Time Series** | $\mathbf{-10.59}$ | $\mathbf{16.23}$ | $\mathbf{0.51}$ | $\mathbf{[-42.40, +21.23]}$ | $\mathbf{0.870}$ | **Level shift indistinguishable from zero.** |
| **Model 5: Placebo (2025-09-20)** | $+24.26$ | $8.09$ | $0.003$ | $[+8.40, +40.11]$ | $0.540$ | A fake event date shows a "significant" jump, so the design produces false positives. |

PM2.5 tells the same story: naive +97.3 µg/m³ ($p<0.001$), ITS +17.0 µg/m³ ($p = 0.59$).

### Conclusions
1. **Acute festival-night pulse:** Hourly data show a sharp 6–12 hour spike on Diwali night (PM2.5 to 960.7 µg/m³; SO₂ tracer 5.3×–7.9× baseline), consistent with substantial firecracker use despite the ban.
2. **Rapid dispersion:** The spike decayed within 18–24 hours.
3. **Weeks-long smog:** The multi-week rise that followed tracks the seasonal trend. Boundary-layer height, inversions and stubble burning are plausible drivers but are **hypotheses**: the project has no PBLH or fire-count data to test them.
4. **Statistical limitation:** Mumbai 2023–2025 control records were not available (Jan–Jul 2026 only), so a cross-city Difference-in-Differences design was not possible.

# 17. Project Story / Narrative

When presenting this project in a portfolio or technical interview, structure the narrative as an engineering journey:

1. **The Problem:** Delhi has severe winter air quality crises, and GRAP actions are often triggered after pollution is already high.
2. **Building the Ground Truth (Data & Engineering):** Raw CPCB/DPCC data had sensor dropouts, sentinel collisions (e.g. 9 °C dropped as a sentinel) and date-integer quirks. Built an idempotent PostgreSQL 16 schema with composite keys, cell-level nullification and JSONB QC flags.
3. **Leakage-Safe Features:** 124 backward-looking features for momentum, diurnal cycles and cross-station synchronization ($r \ge 0.92$), with a written leakage audit.
4. **The Baseline That Is Hard to Beat:** AQI is a 24h rolling average, so "copy the last value" is very strong. Every model is reported against it. At 1h the model does not beat it on RMSE; that is reported, not hidden.
5. **The Extrapolation Wall:** The 24h tree model collapsed (MAE 98.24) because trees cannot predict above the training maximum (~347) and calendar features routed winter rows into monsoon leaves. A 50/50 Persistence + Ridge hybrid brought MAE to 34.11 (+11% skill over persistence), and the gain held in a second winter.
6. **From Forecasts to Decisions:** Mapped forecasts to CPCB tiers and GRAP stages, chose a cost-sensitive alert threshold on validation only, and reported recall with precision and a persistence baseline. Found and removed an earlier test-set leak in threshold selection.
7. **The Policy Question:** A naive comparison says pollution jumped 135 AQI after Diwali. Pre-trend and placebo checks show that design is invalid, and ITS finds no identifiable effect ($p = 0.51$). Hourly tracers still show an acute, short-lived festival-night spike.
8. **What Did Not Work (Tier 2):** More winters, weather features and larger tuning budgets barely moved the 24h forecast; raw PM2.5 is forecastable at 1h/6h. Knowing when to stop is part of the result.
9. **Delivery:** A FastAPI + React dashboard (and the original Streamlit app) over the backtest outputs, plus a cleaned 220,042-row public dataset on Kaggle.
